# Brandvid

The demo video stage of Nomi: a trailer of 20 to 25 seconds with sound, written as one animated page from the product's own interface and rendered frame by frame. `<skill-dir>` is the folder containing `SKILL.md`. It runs when a demo, launch or trailer video is asked for, and as part of a README unless the user said no video. A trailer is edited, like any trailer; what it may not do is show something the product does not do.

## Inputs
- The product's own interface: its markup, CSS, colours, fonts, words. Read the real files; the trailer is built from them, not from memory.
- Its jobs as flows (`.nomi/plan.md` or `.nomi/flows.json`), run until they complete with `review <target>`. The trailer shows only what those flows showed: the same controls, the same messages, the same results.
- What kind of business it is and who watches: a till is shown quick, a dashboard calm.

## Steps
1. Inspect, then plan. The video introduces the product: after one viewing a stranger knows what it is, who it is for, what it does and how to get it.
   - Read the main page, the stylesheets (exact colours and fonts), the README, the data and the messages the product shows. Then answer in a line each: what is it; who is it for and what does it do for them; what sets it apart; which two or three real things in use must be seen (entry, key action, result); the hook; the one line a person would post with it.
   - Storyboard, durations summing to the length. A starting shape, not a template (reorder, merge or drop beats when the product asks for it): **hook** (1.5 to 3 s: the name or the promise, in the product's own words), **reveal** (2 to 4 s: what it is, with its real headline), **two or three highlights** (4 to 5 s each: one real capability in use, with the product's own interface, data and messages), **punchline** (2 to 3 s: the result, as large as it gets), **outro** (2 to 3 s: the name and the command or address to start). A scene of words is under two seconds a line; a highlight lasts while something is happening in it.
   - Only what the product has: its copy, its numbers computed the way its code computes them, its messages word for word. A name typed into a form is allowed; an invented claim, figure or testimonial is not.
2. Write `.nomi/video.html`: one page of exactly 1920x1080, `<html data-duration="20" data-poster="10" data-tone="punchy">` (`tone`: `calm`, `default` or `punchy`; `poster`: the second that is the strongest still picture). Everything inline; an image it uses sits beside it.
   - **What must hold, in every video.** The real product is in it. The pictures already exist: `observe <target> --flow .nomi/flows.json` leaves a photograph of the real page after every step of every flow, `.nomi/runs/<id>/flow-<n>-step-<k>.png` (1440x900; step 0 is the page as it opens). Copy the ones you need beside `video.html`; show a part of one by cropping it in CSS (`object-fit`, `object-position`, or a `background` with a size and a position). Build the highlights from those pictures, with the one number or line a scene is about set beside them as large as a headline, taken from the page as it ran. Text meant to be read stays 0.3 s a word after it has arrived. Numbers are set in lining, equal-width figures and change one place at a time; a figure that rolls sits in a cell with its own line height (cell and line both 1.25em, the strip moving one cell), or its top and bottom are cut. Nothing changes in a single frame by accident, two full layouts are never faded through each other, and no pointer is drawn.
   - **Everything else is yours to design, and should not look like the last video.** Before writing any CSS, write three lines in your notes: the concept (one picture or idea taken from this product and its business, which the whole video plays on), the look (colours and type from the product, and how they are used), the motion (how things arrive, leave and hand over, as a small set of rules with their durations). A café can be a counter where plates are set down and a ticket is torn off; a timer, one shape that fills; a dashboard, a single line that draws itself through every scene; a booking page, a calendar whose days flip. The concept decides the transitions, the framing of the real page (a window, a part lifted out of it, the page filling the picture, a strip under a line of words, at an angle or flat) and what the sound marks.
   - **A new video is a new design.** Asked for another video, or to make it again, start from a new concept: a different picture, different transitions, different music. Asked to fix something in a video ("the tear looks wrong", "too fast here"), fix that and keep the rest of that video as it is.
   - **Judge it by eye, not by rule.** After each render open the sheet and ask of every scene: is it beautiful as a still, is it clear what it says, does it belong to the same film as the scene before, and is there a movement in it I would cut if it were mine? A movement that only shows off (a page flipping up from the floor, something printing in steps) goes. Smooth, with weight, beats clever.
   - **Habits that make a video look generated**, to avoid unless the concept asks for them: the same entrance on every element, cards on a soft gradient with shadows and blur, a window shown flat and frontal scene after scene, a new kind of transition at every cut, text that rises in and lifts out.
   - **Time.** All motion starts at time 0 and is fixed in advance: `element.animate` with delays, and script-driven changes inside `window.nomiVideo = { start() { ... }, cues: [] }` with `setTimeout`. No `Math.random`, no network. An animation that should not show before its time uses `fill: 'forwards'`, or its first state holds from time 0.
   - **Sound.** Push `{ at: seconds, kind }` into `nomiVideo.cues` where things happen: `cut` (a scene opens), `click`, `key`, `tick` (a result), `wow` (the strongest moment, once). The music is made around them, in the character you choose for this product: `nomiVideo.music = { style, bpm, root }` with `style` one of `warm` (soft pad, plucked figure), `cafe` (major, a mellow swung bell and a walking bass), `cinema` (minor, slow and deep), `pulse` (minor, quick and electronic); `bpm` 50 to 160; `root` a MIDI note, 40 to 72. A different product gets different music.
3. Render, from the project folder:
```
node <skill-dir>/scripts/nomi.mjs brandvid compose .nomi/video.html
```
4. Open `sheet.png` and `poster.png`. Judge them as a stranger: is the hook readable at once, is each scene one clear thing, does anything overlap or sit off its control, would a frame be worth posting. Fix the page and render again: at most 3 more rounds. You cannot hear the sound: say so.

## Outputs
- `.nomi/video/demo.mp4` (H.264, 1920x1080, 60 frames a second, with sound; `data-fps="30"` on the page halves the render time for a draft), `poster.png`, `preview.webp` (a short silent loop), `sheet.png`, `video.json`. `--out <dir>` writes them elsewhere; `--no-sound` leaves the sound out. A render takes about three minutes: give the command a timeout of ten minutes, and draft with `data-fps="30"` on the page (half the time), removing it for the last render.
- The check that must pass (the command prints it, exit 1 when it fails): 20 to 30 seconds, no black stretch over 2 seconds and nothing motionless over 4, no script error, a picture of the real product in it (`video.product`), a file that shows what was rendered, a sound track that plays.
- In your reply: the files, the length, the angle in one sentence, what each scene shows, what the check said, and one to three sentences a person could post with the video, specific to this product.

## Stop
- A recording is not this stage's video. "Make a demo video", "a launch video", "a trailer" all mean the trailer above. Only when the user's own words ask for a recording ("record the screen", "film it as it is", "no editing"), write `.nomi/storyboard.json` (`hook`, one or two `scenes` that each name a flow and give a `caption`, one of them with `wow`: the number of its strongest step, `cta`) and run `brandvid <url-or-index.html>`. It replays those flows in a real browser and films them under the page's own clock.
- Done when the check passes and you opened the sheet and the poster.
- Never in a trailer: a control, a number or a message the product does not have; a result no flow produced; a claim the page cannot show.
- No flows complete: this stage hands back to `modules/review.md`. There is no trailer of a product that does not work.
